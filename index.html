October 2026Lab News

NRMO Lab website launches

The Natural Resource Modeling and Observation Lab website is now live! We invite you to meet our team, explore our research on wildfire, water resources, and woody plant encroachment, and discover our latest publications and updates.

October 2026Water Research

New paper examines how afforestation and climate change shape water resources

Former lab member, Dr. Qiong Wu and coauthors published “Responses of Hydrological Processes to Future Climate Variability and Agricultural Land Afforestation in a Humid River Basin” in Ecohydrology. Using SWAT+ simulations in the Lower Mississippi Alluvial Valley, we find that afforestation can increase shallow groundwater recharge while reducing water yield, highlighting trade-offs for forest restoration and water management under a changing climate.

October 2026Wildfire Research

New paper examines future wildfire hazards in the Southern Great Plains

Quan Zhang and coauthors published “Different meteorological drivers govern future extreme fire weather frequency and burn probability in the Southern Great Plains” in Agricultural and Forest Meteorology. The study finds that warming and drying increase extreme fire-weather days, while wind is most strongly associated with changes in simulated burn probability.

October 2026Wildfire Research

Near-real-time Hourly Fire Potential Platform enters testing

We have developed a new version of our near-real-time Hourly Fire Potential Platform for the contiguous United States (CONUS), which is now being tested. The platform provides hourly data on fuel moisture, fire danger, potential fire behavior, and weather to support wildfire management, prescribed burn planning, and assessment of current fire risk.

August 2026Research funding

PhD student Quan Zhang awarded JFSP GRIN funding for grassland wildfire research

Congratulations to Quan Zhang on receiving JFSP GRIN funding! Quan will lead a project using remote sensing to map grassland biomass and fuel loads across Oklahoma and examine how fuel accumulation and extreme fire weather interact to shape wildfire risk. The project runs from August 2026 through July 2027.

July 2026Wildfire Research

New paper introduces a simulation-based framework for wildfire danger forecasting

Our paper, “Wildfire danger assessment and forecast through fire spread simulations,” is published in the International Journal of Wildland Fire. We developed the Wildfire Danger Assessment and Forecast (WDAF) framework, which combines weather, fuel, and terrain data to estimate daily fire spread potential. Demonstrated in the Southern Great Plains, the framework offers a complementary approach to existing fire danger rating systems.

July 2026Wildfire Research

New paper explores prescribed burning to reduce fuel load and wildfire risk in Tallgrass Prairie

Alex Desjardins and coauthors published a new paper in Fire Ecology, “Model-based assessment of prescribed burning frequency to mitigate wildfire risks in tallgrass prairie.” The study combines field measurements in tallgrass prairie with FARSITE fire behavior simulations to examine fuel accumulation and identify prescribed burning frequencies that can reduce future wildfire risk.
