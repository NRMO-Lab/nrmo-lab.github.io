01

Wildfire Monitoring and Drivers

Where, when, and why do wildfires grow rapidly?

Satellite Earth observationHourly fire progressionEnvironmental drivers

2026 · RESEARCH PUBLICATION

Tracking wildfire growth hour by hour

Using GOES satellite observations, we examined hourly progression and environmental drivers of large U.S. wildfires. The study connects patterns of rapid fire growth with weather, terrain, and vegetation, providing observational evidence for understanding fast-moving fires. Our results identify Northern California and the Central Great Plains as two key regions experiencing rapid progression during large wildfires. The fastest recorded progression rate was 320 km²/h on February 27, 2024, during the Smokehouse Creek Fire in Texas.

Publication details

† Fang, S., Yang, J., Jiao, X., Zou, C. B., Desjardins, A., Yang, H., ... & Hernandez, A. (2026). Satellite-based analysis of hourly progression and driving factors of large US wildfires. International Journal of Applied Earth Observation and Geoinformation, 149, 105288.

Read the paper ↗

2015 · RESEARCH PUBLICATION

Understanding the growing role of large fires

We analyzed large-fire activity across the conterminous United States during 1984–2012, examining fire size and burn severity. The study documents the increasing significance of large fires and shows why burn severity matters when assessing their effects. Our work suggests that continued warming and drying during the twenty-first century could increase total burned area and fire emissions by promoting larger and more severe wildfires.

Publication details

Yang, J., Tian, H., Tao, B., Ren, W., Pan, S., Liu, Y., Wang, Y. (2015) A growing importance of large fires in conterminous United States during 1984–2012. Journal of Geophysical Research: Biogeosciences, , 120(12), 2625-2640

Read the paper ↗

2014 · RESEARCH PUBLICATION

Reconstructing global fire history

We reconstructed global burned-area patterns for the twentieth and early twenty-first centuries and examined their relationships with environmental and human influences. The average global burned area is approximately 442 × 10⁴ km² yr⁻¹ during 1901–2007, and our results suggest a notable declining rate of burned area globally (1.28 × 10⁴ km² yr⁻¹).

Publication details

Yang, J., Tian, H., Tao, B., Ren, W., Kush, J., Liu, Y., Wang, Y. (2014) Spatial and temporal patterns of global burned area in response to anthropogenic and environmental factors: Reconstructing global fire history for the 20th and early 21st centuries. Journal of Geophysical Research: Biogeosciences, 119(3), 249-263

02

Fire Danger Assessment and Prediction

What do current fuel and weather conditions mean for potential fire behavior?

Fire-spread simulationsFuel and soil moistureMachine learning

2026 · RESEARCH PUBLICATION

Assessing fire danger through simulated fire spread

We developed a fire danger assessment framework based on fire-spread simulations and simulated fire size. Its application in the Southern Great Plains demonstrates how fire behavior modeling can complement empirical fire danger ratings when evaluating fire potential. This study highlights the potential of simulation-based approaches for wildfire danger assessment and forecasting.

Publication details

Yang, J., Ochsner, T., Krueger, E., Fang, S., Zhang, Q., Jiao, X., ... & Daily, D. (2026). Wildfire Danger Assessment and Forecast Through Fire Spread Simulations. International Journal of Wildland Fire, 35, WF25278.

Read the paper ↗

2025 · RESEARCH PUBLICATION

Resolving soil moisture for wildfire prediction

We developed a downscaled hourly surface soil moisture dataset and explored its use for wildfire prediction across the contiguous United States. The work links hydrological information with fire research by improving the spatial detail of hourly soil moisture estimates.

Publication details

† Yang, H., Wang, Q., Yang, J., Krueger, E. S., Xi, H., Liu, W., ... & Ochsner, T. E. (2025). Generating a downscaled hourly surface soil moisture dataset for wildfire prediction. Journal of Hydrology, 133795.

03

Prescribed Fire and Fuel Management

How can fuel management reduce the potential for severe wildfire behavior?

Field fuel measurementsGrassland biomass mappingFARSITE modeling

2026 · RESEARCH PUBLICATION

Evaluating prescribed-burning frequency in tallgrass prairie

Using field fuel measurements from the Tallgrass Prairie Preserve and FARSITE simulations, we assessed how prescribed-burning frequency influences potential wildfire behavior. The study connects fuel accumulation, fuel height, and fire weather to the evaluation of grassland fuel-management strategies. Our findings suggest that frequent prescribed burning is necessary to reduce the potential for intense future wildfire behavior in tallgrass prairies and other highly productive grasslands.

Publication details

† Desjardins, A., J. Yang, X. Jiao, Q. Zhang, R. DeSantis, R. Sutherland, and S. Fuhlendorf. "Model-based assessment of prescribed burning frequency to mitigate wildfire risks in tallgrass prairie." Fire Ecology (2026).

Future Wildfire Risk under Climate Change

How will changing climates reshape fire danger in the Great Plains?

Climate scenariosFire weather extremesBurn probability

2026 · RESEARCH PUBLICATION

Separating drivers of extreme fire weather and burn probability

We examined future wildfire hazard near Amarillo, Oklahoma City, Dallas, and San Antonio. Warming and drying drive projected increases in extreme fire weather days, while wind speed has the strongest association with changes in burn probability, revealing distinct controls on these two measures of hazard.

Publication details

† Zhang, Q., Yang, J., Fang, S., Desjardins, A., Yang, H., Jiao, X., Zou, C. (2026) Different Meteorological Drivers Govern Future Extreme Fire Weather Frequency and Burn Probability in the Southern Great Plains, Agricultural and Forest Meteorology, 390(15), 111458

Read the paper ↗

2025 · RESEARCH PUBLICATION

Projecting Southern Great Plains fire danger

We investigated how changing climate conditions may alter wildfire danger in the Southern Great Plains throughout the twenty-first century. The study examines future fire weather and fuel conditions to understand potential shifts in the regional fire environment.

Publication details

† Fang, S., Yang, J., Zou, C. B., Krueger, E. S., Ochsner, T. E., & Zhang, Q. (2025). Wildfire danger under changing climates in the southern Great Plains throughout the 21st century. Ecological Indicators, 170, 112994.

05

Fire Impacts and Ecosystem Recovery

How does fire alter ecosystems, and how do landscapes recover?

Post-fire recoveryCarbon emissionsEcosystem feedbacks

2017 · RESEARCH PUBLICATION

Measuring vegetation recovery after fire

Using MODIS observations, we quantified post-fire vegetation greenness recovery across temperate and boreal North America. The study examines variation among vegetation types and ecoregions and relates recovery patterns to vegetation composition and fire severity.

Publication details

Yang, J., Pan, S., Dangal, S., Zhang, B., Wang, S., Tian, H. (2017) Continental-scale quantification of post-fire vegetation greenness recovery in temperate and boreal North America. Remote Sensing of Environment, 199, 277-290

Read the paper ↗

2015 · RESEARCH PUBLICATION

Connecting fire emissions with terrestrial carbon balance

We examined century-scale patterns of global fire-related carbon emissions and the influence of fire on terrestrial carbon balance. The work places fire within the broader carbon cycle, connecting combustion losses with ecosystem carbon dynamics over long timescales.

Publication details

Yang, J, Tian, H., Tao, B., Ren, W., Lu, C., Pan, S., Wang, Y., Liu, Y. (2015) Century-scale patterns and trends of global pyrogenic carbon emissions and fire influences on terrestrial carbon balance. Global Biogeochemical Cycles, 29(9), 1549-1566

Read the paper ↗

2021 · RESEARCH PUBLICATION

Projecting wildfire emissions in the western United States

This collaborative study projects future wildfire emissions in the western United States under climate change. It separates the contributions of changes in burned area, fuel loading, and fuel moisture to clarify how different processes shape future emissions.

Publication details

Liu, Y., Liu, Y., Fu, J., Yang, C., Dong, X., Tian, H., Tao, B., Yang, J., Wang, Y., Zou, Y., and Ke, Z. (2021) Projection of future wildfire emissions in the western United States under climate change: contributions from changes in wildfire, fuel loading, and fuel moisture, International Journal of Wildland Fire. https://doi.org/10.1071/WF20190

Read the paper ↗

2019 · RESEARCH PUBLICATION

Developing the RESFire Earth system model

We contributed to the development of the Region-Specific Ecosystem Feedback Fire model within the Community Earth System Model. RESFire connects fire occurrence, spread, and impacts with natural and human influences, enabling interactive studies of fire, climate, and ecosystems.

Publication details

Zou, Y., Wang, Y., Ke, Z., Tian, H., Yang, J. and Liu, Y., (2019). Development of a REgion‐Specific Ecosystem Feedback Fire (RESFire) Model in the Community Earth System Model. Journal of Advances in Modeling Earth Systems, 11(2), pp.417-445.

Read the paper ↗

2020 · RESEARCH PUBLICATION

Exploring climate–fire–ecosystem interactions

Using CESM-RESFire, this collaborative work investigates interactions among climate, fire, and ecosystems and their implications for decadal climate variability. Coupled modeling provides a way to examine feedbacks that cannot be represented by treating fire and climate as independent processes.

Publication details

Zou, Y., Wang, Y., Qian, Y., Tian, H., Yang, J., Alvarado, E. (2020), Using CESM-RESFire to understand climate-fire-ecosystem interactions and Implications for Decadal Climate Variability, Atmospheric Chemistry and Physics, 20
