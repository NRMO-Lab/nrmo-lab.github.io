Species Distributions and Range Shifts

Where will encroaching woody plants find suitable conditions as climate changes?

Species distribution modelingClimate scenariosSouthern Great Plains

2024 · RESEARCH PUBLICATION

Projecting the future range of eastern redcedar

We examined how future climate conditions may alter the distribution of eastern redcedar in the Southern Great Plains. The study projects an eastward shift in its distribution range, providing a basis for anticipating where climate suitability may change and prioritizing monitoring and management of woody encroachment.

Publication details

Yang, J., Will, R., Zou, C., Zhai, L., Winrich, A. and Fang, S., (2024). Eastward shift in Juniperus virginiana distribution range under future climate conditions in the Southern Great Plains, United States. Agricultural and Forest Meteorology, 345, p.109836.

2024 · RESEARCH PUBLICATION

Anticipating range shifts of encroaching woody species

We investigated how future climate change may shift the ranges of major encroaching woody plant species in the Southern Great Plains. Comparing responses among species helps identify changing areas of potential encroachment and supports planning for early intervention and grassland conservation.

Publication details

Yang, J., Will, R., Zhai, L., & Zou, C. (2024). Future climate change shifts the ranges of major encroaching woody plant species in the Southern Great Plains, USA. Earth's Future, 12(7), e2024EF004520.

Woody Expansion and Water Resources

How does increasing woody cover affect streamflow and water availability?

SWAT+ modelingForest expansionWatershed processes

2024 · RESEARCH PUBLICATION

Connecting woody expansion with streamflow

Using SWAT+ simulations in the Little River Watershed, we examined how forest expansion and future climate conditions influence streamflow. The study highlights declining water availability as forest cover increases and connects woody vegetation management with the sustainability of water resources in subhumid Oklahoma.

Publication details

Yang, J., Winrich, A., Zhang, T., Qiao, L., Mattingly, C., & Zou, C. (2024). Responses of streamflow to forest expansion in a typical subhumid watershed under future climate conditions. Journal of Environmental Management, 357, 120780.

Ecosystem Carbon and Water Trade-offs

How do changing vegetation and woodland maturation alter carbon and water exchange?

Ecosystem productivityCarbon exchangeWater-use efficiency

2026 · RESEARCH PUBLICATION

Comparing juniper woodland with native tallgrass prairie

We examined how the maturation of encroaching juniper woodland changes ecosystem carbon and water exchange relative to native tallgrass prairie. The study links higher gross primary productivity and water use with changes in net ecosystem exchange, demonstrating why the consequences of encroachment must be considered across multiple ecosystem processes.

Publication details

Zhang, T., Zou, C. B., Will, R. E., Ferguson, B., & Yang, J. (2026). Maturation of encroaching juniper woodland elevates gross primary productivity and water use but reduces net ecosystem exchange relative to native tallgrass prairie. Agricultural and Forest Meteorology, 378, 111009.

Evaluating productivity and water-use trade-offs

We investigated trade-offs between ecosystem productivity and water use as tree cover increases under future climate conditions in southcentral United States. The study connects vegetation change with carbon uptake, water demand, and water-use efficiency, informing assessment of the benefits and costs of increasing woody cover.

Publication details

Zhang, T., Yang, J., Winrich, A., Will, R. E., & Zou, C. B. (2024). Trade-off of ecosystem productivity and water use related to afforestation in southcentral USA under climate change. Science of The Total Environment, 915, 170255.

Weather Extremes and Ecosystem Stability

How do grasslands and encroached woodlands respond to droughts and wet spells?

Eddy covarianceWeather extremesEcosystem stability

2026 · RESEARCH PUBLICATION

Understanding ecosystem stability during weather extremes

Using eddy covariance observations from a central Oklahoma tallgrass prairie and an adjacent juniper-encroached woodland, we examined how the timing and sequence of wet spells and flash droughts affect carbon and water fluxes. Responses varied with vegetation type and event sequence, highlighting the importance of native grasslands for ecosystem stability under a more variable climate.

Publication details

Zhang, T., Zou, C. B., Potts, D. L., & Yang, J. (2026). Sequence and Seasonal Timing of Weather Extremes Influence Ecosystem Stability in Tallgrass Prairie and Juniper woodland. Ecosystems, 29(4), 61.
